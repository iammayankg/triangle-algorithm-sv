\title{Guarded Block Transfers for Polytope Distance:\\
Linear Convergence with Explicit Constants and\\
Gap-Certified Screening for SVM Training}
\author{Mayank Gupta \and Bahman Kalantari}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Hard-margin SVM training is the distance problem between two convex
hulls, and the Triangle Algorithm (TA) solves it with a certified
duality gap from supporting hyperplanes. We study the family of
weight-transfer steps behind the enhanced TA and introduce
\emph{guarded block transfers}: $k$ MDM-style transfers per scan,
aggregated into one exact line search, guarded by an $O(1)$ comparison
against the single best transfer, with an away-step fallback on
capacity-clipped iterations. Analysing the method on the
Minkowski-difference polytope---where the objective is $1$-strongly
convex and a single pyramidal width governs the geometry---we prove
global linear convergence with explicit constants and \emph{no}
combinatorial swap-step bounds: the away fallback turns every
potentially bad iteration into either a guaranteed contraction or a
support-shrinking drop step. We further prove that the TA's certified
gap equals the Frank--Wolfe gap over that polytope divided by the
current distance, and use it to show that gap-certified safe screening
removes every non-support point after an explicit
$O\!\left(\tfrac{k}{\rho}\log\tfrac{1}{\tau}\right)$ iterations, where
$\tau$ is the optimal-face score margin. Every inequality is verified
numerically at machine precision. Experiments on synthetic and real
data delineate the regime the theory predicts: on sparse-support
problems (hard margins, kernels, high dimension) the optimised solver
is $1.6$--$2.5\times$ faster to a certified solution than LIBSVM, while
on dense-support $L_2$ soft margins the $1/n$ pyramidal width makes
primal coordinate descent the right tool---a boundary the analysis
anticipates and the experiments confirm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Training a linear hard-margin SVM is equivalent to finding the closest
points of the two classes' convex hulls: the optimal separator is the
perpendicular bisector of the witness pair, and the margin is half the
hull distance \cite{bennett2000duality}. Kalantari's Triangle Algorithm
(TA) exploits this \emph{distance duality} directly \cite{kalantari2015},
maintaining an iterate pair $(p,q)$ and, crucially, a certified interval
$[\LB,\UB]$ on the distance from parallel supporting hyperplanes, so it
can stop at a provable relative accuracy $\varepsilon$. The enhanced TA
of \cite{gupta2026} adds joint segment updates, dot-product caching, an
anti-zig-zag heuristic and prioritised search, and reports large
speedups over SMO on synthetic data.

The workhorse step of such geometric solvers is the \emph{weight
transfer}: move mass from the worst active vertex to the best candidate
vertex---the Mitchell--Demyanov--Malozemov (MDM) step
\cite{mdm1974}, which is exactly the pairwise Frank--Wolfe (FW) step of
\cite{lj2015} on the relevant polytope. Each such step costs an $O(n)$
scan for one transfer. This paper asks how much of that scan can be
amortised, and what it costs in guarantees.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \textbf{Guarded block transfers} (\S\ref{sec:alg}): pair the top-$k$
receivers with the worst-$k$ active donors, take one exact line search
along the aggregated direction, and \emph{guard} by an $O(1)$ comparison
with the single MDM step; on capacity-clipped iterations, fall back to
an away step. Cache updates for a whole block are one GEMV.
\item \textbf{Linear convergence with explicit constants}
(\S\ref{sec:theory}, Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}): by working on the
Minkowski-difference polytope $Z=\conv(V)-\conv(W)$, the guarded block
algorithm inherits the pyramidal-width rate of pairwise FW with a
constant factor, and the away fallback replaces pairwise FW's
combinatorial swap-step bound with linear drop-step counting.
\item \textbf{The certified gap is the FW gap}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gapid}), which yields a \emph{screening activation}
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:activation}): gap-certified safe screening
provably removes all non-support points after an explicit number of
iterations.
\item \textbf{A regime characterisation} (\S\ref{sec:exp}) rather than a
speed claim: the theory predicts an $O(n^2)$-type cost when the optimal
support is dense ($\PW \sim 1/n$), and both synthetic and real
experiments show exactly that boundary---with the solver winning against
LIBSVM on sparse-support problems and losing to LIBLINEAR on
dense-support $L_2$ soft margins.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

Let $V=\{v_1,\dots,v_n\}$, $W=\{w_1,\dots,w_m\}\subset\R^d$. The
polytope distance problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
  \min_{p\in\conv(V),\ q\in\conv(W)} \ \tfrac12\norm{p-q}^2 .
\end{equation}
Writing $z=p-q$ and $Z:=\conv(V)-\conv(W)=\conv\{v_i-w_j\}$,
\eqref{eq:P} is the minimum-norm-point problem $\min_{z\in Z}F(z)$,
$F(z)=\tfrac12\norm z^2$, whose objective is $1$-strongly convex and
$1$-smooth. Let $z^*$ be the minimiser, $\delta^*=\norm{z^*}$,
$h(z)=F(z)-F(z^*)$, $M=\operatorname{diam}(Z)$ and
$\delta=\PW(Z)>0$ the pyramidal width \cite{lj2015}.

The algorithm keeps simplex weights $\alpha$ on $V$, $\beta$ on $W$,
with $p=\sum\alpha_iv_i$, $q=\sum\beta_jw_j$; the induced product
representation $\lambda_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_j$ of $z$ over the atoms
$v_i-w_j$ has support $\operatorname{supp}\alpha\times\operatorname{supp}\beta$.
With $g=-\nabla F(z)=q-p$, the \emph{side scores} are
$s^V_i=\ip{g}{v_i}$ and $s^W_j=\ip{p-q}{w_j}$; the implementation
maintains them incrementally as differences of cached arrays
$V\!p,V\!q,Wp,Wq$.

\paragraph{Certificate.} For any iterate, $\UB=\norm z$ and
$\LB=\min_{u\in Z}\ip{z}{u}/\norm z=(\min_i\ip{z}{v_i}-\max_j\ip{z}{w_j})/\UB$
satisfy $\LB\le\delta^*\le\UB$; the TA stops when
$\UB-\LB\le\varepsilon\,\UB$. The $L_2$ soft margin, $\nu$-SVM and
kernel variants reduce to \eqref{eq:P} exactly (augmented coordinates
$K+I/C$; reduced convex hulls; feature-space images) and are covered by
everything below; details in the appendix.

\paragraph{Gaps.} $\gFW=\max_{u\in Z}\ip{g}{u}-\ip{g}{z}$,
$\gA=\ip{g}{z}-\min_{u\in\operatorname{supp}\lambda}\ip{g}{u}$,
$\gPW=\gFW+\gA$. Because $\ip{g}{v_i-w_j}=s^V_i+s^W_j$ is separable and
the support is a product set, each gap splits into a $V$-side and a
$W$-side term computable from the score arrays in $O(n+m)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sep}), and a transfer $v_r-v_u$ on the $V$ side is a
pairwise direction of $Z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:side}).

\section{Guarded block transfers}\label{sec:alg}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One iteration of the guarded block-transfer step (side $V$; $W$ symmetric)}
\label{alg:block}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State top-$k$ receivers by $s^V$; worst-$k$ donors in $\operatorname{supp}\alpha$; pair best-with-worst: $(r_j,u_j)$, $j\le k'$; pair 1 is the MDM pair $(r^*,u^*)$
\State $d_j\gets v_{r_j}-v_{u_j}$;\ $\mathrm{gap}_j\gets s^V_{r_j}-s^V_{u_j}$;\ $\gamma_j\gets\min(\mathrm{gap}_j/\norm{d_j}^2,\ c_j)$ \Comment{$c_j$: donor capacity}
\If{pair 1 uncapped ($\gamma_1=\mathrm{gap}_1/\norm{d_1}^2$)}
  \State $\Delta_B\gets$ gain of exact line search $t\in[0,1]$ along $d=\sum_j\gamma_jd_j$;\quad $\Delta_1\gets$ gain of the MDM step
  \State take the step with the larger gain \Comment{guard, $O(1)$}
\Else
  \If{the away step on $u^*$ is boundary-clipped} take it \Comment{drop step: $\alpha_{u^*}\!\to0$}
  \Else\ take the best of \{block, MDM, away($u^*$), toward($r^*$)\}
  \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:block} amortises one $O(n)$ scan over up to $k$
transfers. Every candidate gain is available in $O(1)$ from cached
quantities: with $S=\ip{g}{d}$ and $D=\norm d^2$, the block gain is
$tS-t^2D/2$ at $t=\min(S/D,1)$, where $S=\sum_j\gamma_j\mathrm{gap}_j$
and $D=\gamma^\top G\gamma$ for the $k'\times k'$ Gram matrix $G$ of the
pair directions, read off the cached columns; the MDM, away and toward
gains are the standard one-dimensional expressions. The cache update
for the whole block is a single matrix--vector product with the net
per-index coefficients. Feasibility is immediate: donors lose at most
$t\gamma_j\le c_j$, receivers only gain, and the weight sum is conserved.

\paragraph{Safe screening.} With $r=\sqrt{\UB^2-\max(\LB,0)^2}$ and
$v_{\min}=\arg\min_i\ip{z}{v_i}$, a zero-weight point with
$\ip{z}{v_i}-\ip{z}{v_{\min}}>r\norm{v_i-v_{\min}}$ is outside the
optimal support and is removed (symmetrically on $W$ with the max).
Because the optimum's support survives, the reduced problem has the
same optimum and all subsequent lower bounds remain valid.

\section{Analysis}\label{sec:theory}

Full proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}; we state the results and
the ideas.

\begin{lemma}[Block gain]\label{lem:block}
Let $b_j=\gamma_j\norm{d_j}$. Then $S\ge\sum_jb_j^2$, $D\le k'S$, and
the realised block gain satisfies
$\Delta_B\ge S/(2k')\ge\Delta_{\mathrm{MDM}}/(2k')$.
\end{lemma}
The proof is the triangle inequality on $\norm d$ and Cauchy--Schwarz
on $(\sum b_j)^2$; capacity clipping only helps. With the guard, every
case-(a) iteration's gain is $\max(\Delta_B,\Delta_1)\ge\Delta_1$: the
guarded step never makes less progress than pairwise FW.

\begin{lemma}[Good-step progress]\label{lem:good}
Work on the side with the larger pairwise gap. On any non-drop
iteration, $\mathrm{gain}\ge\tfrac1{16}\min\!\big(\gPW{}^2/(2M^2),\ \gPW/2\big)$.
\end{lemma}
Case (a) uses $\gPW_{\text{side}}\ge\gPW/2$ and the exact line search
bound; case (b) uses $\gPW_{\text{side}}=\gFW_{\text{side}}+\gA_{\text{side}}$,
so one of the toward step (always feasible with step $\le1$) or the
interior away step realises at least a quarter of $\gPW$.

\begin{theorem}[Global linear convergence]\label{thm:linear}
Run the guarded block-transfer algorithm with away fallback, any
$k\ge1$, from any vertex pair (initial support $s_0\le2$). Then every
non-drop iteration satisfies $h_{t+1}\le(1-\rho)h_t$ with
$\rho=c\,\delta^2/M^4$ for an explicit absolute constant $c$, the
number of drop steps is at most $k\,T_{\mathrm{good}}+s_0$, and hence
\[
h_T\ \le\ h_0\,(1-\rho)^{(T-s_0)/(k+2)} .
\]
\end{theorem}
The rate follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:good} and the geometric
strong-convexity bound $\gPW(z)^2\ge(\delta^2/2M^2)\,h(z)$ of
\cite{lj2015} applied to $F$ over $Z$. The drop counting is the
away-step argument: each drop removes one support index, non-drop
steps add at most $k$, so drops are at most $k$ times the good steps
plus $s_0$. There is no swap-step bound because case (b) never takes a
capacity-clipped pairwise step---it takes an away step, which either
contracts or drops.

\begin{proposition}[Near-orthogonal speed-up]\label{prop:orth}
If $|\ip{d_i}{d_j}|\le\eta\norm{d_i}\norm{d_j}$ for $i\ne j$, the
interior block gain is at least $(\sum_j\Delta_j)/(1+\eta(k'-1))$,
where $\Delta_j$ is pair $j$'s individual gain.
\end{proposition}
As $\eta\to0$ a single scan realises the sum of all $k$ pairwise gains;
in high dimension distinct support directions are nearly orthogonal,
which is the mechanism behind the measured $4$--$30\times$ iteration
reductions.

\begin{lemma}[The certified gap is the FW gap]\label{lem:gapid}
At any full scan, $\UB-\LB=\gFW(z)/\UB$, and
$\gFW(z)\le2h+R\sqrt{2h}$ with $R=\max_{u\in Z}\norm u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Screening activation]\label{thm:activation}
Assume $\delta^*>0$, let $F_V,F_W$ be the optimal faces,
$\tau>0$ the minimal nonzero score margin
$\ip{z^*}{v_i}-\min_j\ip{z^*}{v_j}$ (and symmetrically on $W$), and
$D$ the larger class diameter. A zero-weight point with margin $\tau_i$
is screened as soon as $r\le\tau_i/(3D)$; consequently, once
$h_t\le\tau^4\delta^{*2}/(2592\,R^4D^4)$---which under
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} holds for all
$t\ge s_0+\tfrac{k+2}{\rho}\log\!\big(2592R^4D^4h_0/(\tau^4\delta^{*2})\big)$---every
zero-weight point outside $F_V\cup F_W$ is removed permanently, and
each later scan costs $O(|F|+|\operatorname{supp}|)$ instead of $O(n+m)$.
\end{theorem}
The $\tau$-dependence is unavoidable: no rule that is safe for all
instances consistent with a certificate of gap $\sim\tau$ can
distinguish a point of margin $\tau$ from a support point.

\paragraph{Numerical verification.} Lemma~\ref{lem:block} was checked on
$4{,}000$ random ensembles including heavily clipped and boundary cases
(worst slack at machine precision), Proposition~\ref{prop:orth} on
$500$ near-orthogonal ensembles, Lemma~\ref{lem:gapid} to $10^{-9}$ at
arbitrary iterates, and Theorem~\ref{thm:activation} on instances whose
optimal face was computed by an exact QP: at every activating round all
zero-weight non-face points were screened, and the final working set
equalled the optimal face exactly. Over $5{,}000$ instrumented live
block steps none increased the objective.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

All solvers are validated against an exact QP (SLSQP over the two
simplices) on small instances---distances agree to $10^{-9}$ or better
for every variant. Synthetic instances follow the protocol of
\cite{gupta2026} (two Gaussian clouds, $n=5{,}000$ per class, separation
$k\times$diameter); stopping rule $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ throughout
unless stated. Hardware for the real-data runs: a dedicated 32-vCPU
Intel Xeon 2.6\,GHz Studio (122\,GB, OpenBLAS, one BLAS thread per
solver).

\paragraph{Step ablation.} At $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ on a
$d{=}30$, $n{=}200$ instance, plain toward steps need $158{,}941$
iterations and the midpoint heuristic of \cite{gupta2026} $148{,}996$;
away steps need $106$ and pairwise $181$. On $d{=}100$, $n{=}1{,}000$
neither toward nor midpoint converge in $300{,}000$ iterations, while
away and pairwise converge in $15{,}886$ and $3{,}921$. Block transfers
then cut iterations a further $4$--$30\times$ relative to single MDM
(e.g.\ $43{,}486\to1{,}551$ on a dense-support $L_2$ instance at
$d{=}1{,}000$) and are the only configuration that reaches the certified
tolerance on MNIST odd-vs-even. At the paper's working tolerance
$10^{-3}$ the pairwise/away gain is only $\sim\!11\%$, which explains why
the midpoint heuristic sufficed in the original study.

\paragraph{Shrinking.} At $\varepsilon=10^{-5}$ on the separated
protocol, screening reduces $10{,}000$ points to $36$--$1{,}054$
survivors and wall-clock by $1.4$--$2.4\times$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:shrink});
at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ runs end before the certificate activates and
it is neutral---exactly Theorem~\ref{thm:activation}'s prediction.

\paragraph{Sparse-support regime: against standard solvers.}
Figure~\ref{fig:final} summarises seven problem classes. On hard

\paragraph{Reading the boundary.} The two batteries together are one
statement: where the optimal support is sparse---hard margins, high
dimension, kernels, $\nu$-SVM at moderate $\nu$, and linearly separable
real datasets---the geometric solver with certificates is competitive
or faster than LIBSVM; where the support is dense, the pyramidal width
of the near-simplex collapses and primal coordinate descent wins by
orders of magnitude. The theory names the quantity that decides
($\PW(Z)$, equivalently support density), and the certificate makes the
diagnosis available at run time.

\section{Related work}
Distance duality and the TA \cite{kalantari2015,gupta2026}; MDM
\cite{mdm1974} and Gilbert's algorithm; away steps
\cite{guelat1986} and the pyramidal-width analysis of FW variants
\cite{lj2015}, whose pairwise step our MDM pair coincides with; the
NPA/reduced-hull view of soft margins \cite{bennett2000duality};
block-coordinate FW \cite{lacoste2013bcfw}, which blocks over
\emph{coordinates} of a product domain rather than aggregating pairs on
one polytope; safe screening for SVMs \cite{elghaoui2012,ogawa2013},
which our activation theorem quantifies for the polytope-distance
certificate; and the SMO/LIBSVM/LIBLINEAR solvers used as baselines.

\section{Conclusion}
Guarded block transfers give the Triangle Algorithm a linear rate with
explicit constants, no combinatorial swap-step bounds, and a
provably-effective screening rule, at the price of a $(k+2)$ factor in
the exponent that the near-orthogonal regime more than repays. The same
analysis predicts, and the experiments confirm, where the method stops
being competitive. Limitations: the implementation is NumPy-level (a
compiled inner loop would shift the practical boundary but not its
$O(n^2)$ asymptotics), sparse-matrix inputs are not yet supported, and
the constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} are loose.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Separability]\label{lem:sep}
With the product representation, $\gPW=\gPW_V+\gPW_W$ where
$\gPW_V=\max_is^V_i-\min_{i\in\operatorname{supp}\alpha}s^V_i\ge0$ and
$\gPW_W$ likewise; the same holds for $\gFW$ and $\gA$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\ip{g}{v_i-w_j}=s^V_i+s^W_j$ is separable and the support is a product
set, so the max over atoms and the min over the support separate; each
side term is nonnegative since the support max dominates the support
min and the global max dominates the support max.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Side transfers are $Z$-pairwise directions]\label{lem:side}
For $\alpha_u>0$ and any $b\in\operatorname{supp}\beta$,
$v_r-v_u=(v_r-w_b)-(v_u-w_b)$ is a difference of an atom of $Z$ and an
atom in the support, and the transfer $\gamma\le\alpha_u$ realises
$z\leftarrow z+\gamma(v_r-v_u)$ inside $Z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:block}]
Since $\gamma_j\le\mathrm{gap}_j/\norm{d_j}^2$ we have
$\gamma_j\mathrm{gap}_j\ge\gamma_j^2\norm{d_j}^2=b_j^2$, so
$S=\sum_j\gamma_j\mathrm{gap}_j\ge\sum_jb_j^2$. By the triangle
inequality $\norm d\le\sum_j\gamma_j\norm{d_j}=\sum_jb_j$ and by
Cauchy--Schwarz $(\sum_jb_j)^2\le k'\sum_jb_j^2\le k'S$, hence
$D\le k'S$. Exact line search on the $1$-smooth $F$ gives
$\Delta_B=S^2/(2D)\ge S/(2k')$ in the interior case and
$\Delta_B=S-D/2\ge S/2$ at the boundary $t=1$ (where $D\le S$). Finally
$S\ge\gamma_1\mathrm{gap}_1\ge\Delta_{\mathrm{MDM}}$ since all terms are
nonnegative and $\Delta_{\mathrm{MDM}}=\gamma_1\mathrm{gap}_1-\gamma_1^2\norm{d_1}^2/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:good}]
Let $g_S=\max(\gPW_V,\gPW_W)\ge\gPW/2$. For a feasible direction with
directional derivative $G$ and maximal step $T$, exact line search on a
$1$-smooth function yields $\mathrm{gain}\ge\min(G^2/(2\norm d^2),\,TG/2)$.
Case (a): the guard's gain is at least the MDM gain, whose derivative is
$g_S$ and whose interior optimum is attained, so
$\mathrm{gain}\ge g_S^2/(2M^2)\ge\gPW{}^2/(8M^2)$. Case (b), non-drop:
$g_S=\gFW_{\text{side}}+\gA_{\text{side}}$, so one term is
$\ge g_S/2\ge\gPW/4$. If it is the FW term, the toward step (maximal
step $1$) gives $\mathrm{gain}\ge\min(\gFW_{\text{side}}{}^2/(2M^2),\gFW_{\text{side}}/2)$;
if it is the away term, the away line search is interior (non-drop) and
$\mathrm{gain}\ge\gA_{\text{side}}{}^2/(2M^2)$. Inserting $\gPW/4$ and
absorbing constants gives the statement with the factor $1/16$ (the
weakest branch, $1/32$, is dominated after the normalisation
$h\le M^2/2$).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}]
Geometric strong convexity \cite[Thm.~6]{lj2015} for the $1$-strongly
convex $F$ over $Z$ gives $\gPW(z)^2\ge(\delta^2/2M^2)\,h(z)$. With
Lemma~\ref{lem:good}, a non-drop step gains at least
$\gPW{}^2/(32M^2)\ge(\delta^2/64M^4)\,h$ in the quadratic branch, and
the linear branch is dominated for $h\le M^2/2$, which always holds
since $h_0\le\norm{z_0}^2/2\le M^2/2$ and $h$ is monotone. Hence
$h_{t+1}\le(1-\rho)h_t$ with $\rho=c\,\delta^2/M^4$. Drop counting: a
drop removes one support index and adds none; a non-drop step adds at
most $k$ and removes at most $k$; the support is always $\ge2$ and
starts at $s_0$, so the number of removals, hence of drops, is at most
$kT_{\mathrm{good}}+s_0$, giving $T_{\mathrm{good}}\ge(T-s_0)/(k+1)$ and
the displayed bound with slack for the unanalysed side. Every step,
drops included, is a descent step, so $h$ is monotone throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:orth}]
$D=\sum_{i,j}\gamma_i\gamma_j\ip{d_i}{d_j}\le\sum_jb_j^2+\eta\sum_{i\ne j}b_ib_j\le(1+\eta(k'-1))\sum_jb_j^2\le(1+\eta(k'-1))S$
by Cauchy--Schwarz on the cross terms; then
$\Delta_B=S^2/(2D)\ge S/(2(1+\eta(k'-1)))$, and for uncapped pairs
$S=\sum_j2\Delta_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gapid}]
$\LB=\min_{u\in Z}\ip{z}{u}/\norm z$ by definition of the supporting
hyperplane bound and $\UB=\norm z$; subtracting,
$\UB-\LB=(\ip{z}{z}-\min_u\ip{z}{u})/\norm z=\gFW/\UB$. At the optimum
$0=\ip{z^*}{z^*}-\min_u\ip{z^*}{u}$; subtracting from $\gFW(z)$,
$\gFW(z)=(\norm z^2-\norm{z^*}^2)+(\min_u\ip{z^*}{u}-\min_u\ip{z}{u})\le2h+\max_u\ip{z^*-z}{u}\le2h+R\norm{z-z^*}\le2h+R\sqrt{2h}$
by $1$-strong convexity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:activation}]
Strong convexity gives $\norm{z-z^*}\le r$ for the screening radius
$r=\sqrt{\UB^2-\max(\LB,0)^2}$. Let $m^*=\min_j\ip{z^*}{v_j}$ and pick
$v'\in F_V$; since $v_{\min}$ minimises $\ip{z}{\cdot}$,
$\ip{z^*}{v_{\min}}-m^*=\ip{z}{v_{\min}-v'}+\ip{z^*-z}{v_{\min}-v'}\le rD$.
Hence $\ip{z}{v_i-v_{\min}}=\ip{z^*}{v_i-v_{\min}}+\ip{z-z^*}{v_i-v_{\min}}\ge\tau_i-rD-r\norm{v_i-v_{\min}}$,
and the rule fires when this exceeds $r\norm{v_i-v_{\min}}$, for which
$\tau_i\ge3rD$ suffices. For the activation time,
$r^2=(\UB-\LB)(\UB+\LB)\le2R(\UB-\LB)=2R\gFW/\UB\le(2R/\delta^*)(2h+R\sqrt{2h})\le(4R^2/\delta^*)\sqrt{2h}$
for $h\le R^2/2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gapid}); requiring
$r^2\le\tau^2/(9D^2)$ yields the stated condition on $h_t$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} with $\log(1/(1-\rho))\ge\rho$ converts it into
the iteration bound. Permanence follows from the safety of the rule.
\end{proof}
