What was implemented

The Triangle Algorithm treats hard-margin SVM as a geometry problem on the convex hulls K, K′ of the two classes. An iterate pair (p, p′) moves by pivots: v ∈ V is a pivot for p when d(p, v) ≥ d(p′, v); if neither class has a pivot, (p, p′) is a witness pair whose perpendicular bisector separates the hulls. TA I decides intersection vs. separation; TA II computes the hull distance by closing the gap between the upper bound d(p, p′) and a lower bound from parallel supporting hyperplanes orthogonal to h = p − p′.

All four of the paper's enhancements are implemented and switchable: joint closest-point updates of the segments [p, v] and [p′, v′]; incremental dot-product caching (O(nd) → O(n) per iteration for revisited pivots); an anti-zig-zag midpoint pivot; and prioritized search over previously used points. The baseline is a hard-margin SMO (maximal-violating-pair selection, C = ∞) in two variants: with an LRU kernel-row cache, and uncached — the cost profile of the original MATLAB baseline.

Correctness was verified against exact QP ground truth (SLSQP over the two probability simplices): ETA and SMO both agree with the QP distance to 10⁻⁵–10⁻¹³ relative error on small instances, and an ablation confirms every enhancement configuration reaches the same distance — in 36 iterations with all enhancements on vs. 327 with all off.

Extension: beyond the midpoint strategy

Since TA II's toward-steps are Frank-Wolfe/Gilbert-type updates, the FW literature offers stronger zig-zag remedies than the paper's midpoint pivot. Two are now implemented as zigzag_strategy options, reusing the weight bookkeeping the algorithm already maintains: away steps (shrink the worst active vertex's weight, Guélat–Marcotte) and pairwise steps (transfer weight from the worst active vertex to the best pivot — the MDM update). Solved to ε = 10⁻⁾:

At tight tolerance the midpoint remedy is nearly ineffective — oscillation re-forms on the new face — while weight-transfer steps cut iterations by two to three orders of magnitude and land closer to the optimum, consistent with the linear convergence of away-step and pairwise Frank-Wolfe over polytopes. At the paper's working tolerance of 10⁻³ the gain is a modest ~11%, which explains why the midpoint heuristic sufficed in the original study. Notably, the paper's own joint closest-point update already solves the classic pathological case (optimum interior to an edge) in a handful of iterations — it is itself a strong anti-zig-zag device.

Extension: optimized ETA vs standard solvers

The paper's future-work section asks for comparisons with up-to-date solvers. The optimized ETA (pairwise strategy) was run against LIBSVM (sklearn SVC, linear kernel), LIBLINEAR (LinearSVC, hinge loss, tuned to recover the geometric margin), our SMO, and a stochastic-subgradient baseline, on the Table-3 protocol; accuracy is each solver's margin 2/‖w‖ against ETA's certified ε = 10⁻⁾ reference interval.

LIBLINEAR is speed-competitive at scale but its margin accuracy degrades past the tolerance at d = 10,000, and it recovers the geometric margin at all only under careful settings (hinge loss, moderate C — squared hinge or very large C misestimate it by 30%+). On this synthetic protocol the paper's thesis holds against modern baselines, not just the original MATLAB SMO.

Extension: soft-margin (L2) SVM

The Triangle Algorithm extends exactly to the L2 soft-margin SVM through the classical reduction: min ½‖w‖² + (C/2)Σξᵢ² is a hard-margin problem in the augmented space x̃ᵢ = [xᵢ; eᵢ/√C], whose Gram matrix is K + I/C and whose hulls never intersect. The augmentation is never materialised — the extra coordinates are exactly the convex weights the algorithm already tracks, so every augmented dot product is a cached product plus a sparse diagonal correction. Validated against the exact QP on the explicit augmentation to ~10⁻¹⁰, with a monotone regularisation path.

Soft margins change the geometry: the support is dense (1,000+ support vectors), and toward-steps stall — oscillation spreads across many vertices, so cycle detection rarely fires. The pairwise machinery from the zig-zag extension becomes the algorithm: step_mode='mdm' makes weight transfer the primary step. Wherever both converge, ETA-mdm and LIBLINEAR agree on the primal objective to six significant figures and on held-out accuracy to 3–4 decimals — the solutions coincide. The high-dimensional, nearly-separable regime is where the geometric approach's advantage lives, for soft margins just as for hard ones; genuinely overlapping data at large C is hard for every dual method as the problem approaches the infeasible hard margin.

Extension: L1 soft margin via reduced hulls

The L1/ν-SVM is the nearest-point problem between reduced convex hulls (Bennett–Bredensteiner; Crisp–Burges): R(V, μ) caps each point's weight at μ = 2/(νl), shrinking the hull toward its centroid — slack as geometry. Two changes adapt the Triangle Algorithm: the extreme point becomes a capped top-k blend (partial sort, still O(n)), and the primary step is the capped MDM transfer, clipped to the box. Validated against the box-constrained QP to 10⁻⁹ and against sklearn's NuSVC (LIBSVM) to ~10⁻¹⁰ via δ = ‖w‖/S with S the per-class dual-coefficient sum. Points at the cap are exactly the margin violators.

Assumptions and caveats

The source study leaves the sampling law, trial count, and k-grid unspecified, so those were reconstructed: uniform-in-unit-ball clouds for the intersection regime, Gaussian clouds (which reproduce the reported distance scales) with k = 1.2 for the dimension sweep and k ∈ [1.2, 1.9] for the sensitivity sweep — the latter lands on distances 54.7–89.8 against the paper's 45.5–78.4. One finding worth noting: the size of the speedup depends on SMO implementation quality. An LRU kernel-row cache alone shrinks TA's edge from 3.7× to 1.5× at d = 10,000 — which supports the paper's own recommendation to extend comparisons to modern solvers such as LIBSVM and ThunderSVM.
